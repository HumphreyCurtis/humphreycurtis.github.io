Hi there 👋. I am a Research Associate at King’s College London, working across human-computer interaction, accessibility and artificial intelligence. I design, build and evaluate human-centred AI systems—including wearable and screenless interfaces—for health, accessibility and public services. Drawing on participatory design, software development and mixed-methods evaluation, I develop new ways for people to communicate, navigate services and work with AI.

I completed my PhD in the Human-Centred Computing Research Group, supervised by Dr Timothy Neate and Dr Rita Borgo, where I co-designed wearable AAC with people with aphasia. Previously, I interned with the Ability Team at Microsoft Research under Dr Martez Mott, designing and evaluating LLM-powered meeting agents for chronically ill people. I now develop Advokit, an inclusive GenAI toolkit co-designed with people with aphasia to support access to public services.

Outside research, I enjoy golf, reading, film and travel. I spend plenty of time walking my family’s three dogs and remain a long-suffering Manchester United supporter.

Selected Projects

AI for Public Services

Advokit

Digital welfare services exclude many people with aphasia. As a Research Associate, I built and deployed Advokit, a schema-driven GenAI toolkit with LLM drafting and safety monitoring. A three-month deployment reached 800 visitors and 2,849 page views, exposed LLM failures and informed its text-box-free redesign and eventual UK-wide Stroke Association adoption.
Discreet Communication Supports

Wearable and Screenless AAC

Conventional AAC can interrupt conversation and draw unwanted attention. Across four years of PhD co-design with 200+ stroke survivors, therapists and clinicians, I built smartwatch, e-ink badge, mixed-reality smartglasses and hearable AAC systems, including on-device ML. The research identified the most viable form factors, earned publication awards and contributed to a £2m UKRI Future Leaders Fellowship.
Human–AI Interaction

AI Meeting Agents for Video Calls

Video calls can intensify fatigue, brain fog and pain for chronically ill people. During my Microsoft Research internship, I led an N=55 survey and used Ditto to co-design LLM-powered meeting agents. The research produced eight design recommendations and a first-authored CHI 2025 paper.
