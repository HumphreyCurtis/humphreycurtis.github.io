Turrellian LED Lamp

Year 2020
Context MSc Computer Science · University of Bristol
Role Researcher · Designer · Developer · Fabricator
Methods Physical prototyping · Affective computing · Pilot evaluation
Technology M5Stack ESP32 · WS2811 LEDs · MQTT · JavaScript
Code Read thesis

Turrellian was my 2020 MSc Computer Science project at the University of Bristol: a hand-built, networked floor lamp that translated reflective mood input into programmable colour sequences. Inspired by James Turrell's immersive light installations, it explored whether ambient light could become an affective interface rather than another manually controlled smart device.

From sketch to connected light

The physical prototype began as a 1.5–2 metre corner lamp designed to wash two walls with colour. A timber body housed addressable LEDs, while the surrounding room became part of the display.

A browser-based journal captured a person's current and desired mood, then sent a small JSON message over MQTT. An M5Stack ESP32 received it and selected one of two programmed lighting behaviours: activating sequences for focused, excited or happy states; slower sequences for contented, serene or relaxed states.

Bottom line

The evaluation was necessarily exploratory, particularly during the disruption of 2020, and the lamp should not be read as a clinical intervention. Its value was as an end-to-end affective-computing prototype: combining interaction design, connected software, embedded programming and physical fabrication in one working system.
